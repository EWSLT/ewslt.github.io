Workshop Overview

Despite rapid progress in speech and language technologies, evaluation remains fundamentally fragmented. There is no unified framework where automatic speech recognition (ASR), text-to-speech (TTS), machine translation (MT), natural language generation, large language models (LLMs), and speech-to-speech systems can be evaluated in a consistent and comparable manner. Each domain relies on its own metrics and benchmarks, making it difficult to measure progress holistically or understand how improvements generalize across modalities.

Existing benchmarks often fail to capture real-world linguistic and communicative phenomena such as disfluencies, code-mixing, morphological richness, dialectal variation, pragmatics, and noisy or spontaneous inputs. In both speech and text systems, evaluation has increasingly become a narrow optimization over proxy metrics (e.g., WER, BLEU, ROUGE), rather than a meaningful assessment of understanding, generation quality, or interaction. This has led to a widening gap between system capabilities and our ability to reliably measure, interpret, and meaningfully ground their behavior.

EWSLT aims to address this gap by bringing together researchers and practitioners working across speech and textual NLP onto a common platform to rethink Evaluation as a First-Class Research Problem. The workshop focuses on developing unified, standardized, interpretable, and linguistically grounded evaluation frameworks that span modalities. This includes new benchmarks, datasets, metrics, and evaluation methodologies for evaluating speech systems, text-based models, and multimodal architectures in a coherent and principled manner.

The workshop will include invited talks, paper presentations, panel discussions, and shared evaluation challenges aimed at addressing fundamental bottlenecks in evaluating modern AI systems, including robustness, generalization, faithfulness, and alignment across speech and language technologies.

Call for Papers

We invite submissions on novel evaluation methodologies, benchmarks, datasets, and analysis of speech and language technologies, with a strong emphasis on moving beyond conventional metrics such as WER, BLEU, and MOS. Submissions must follow the ACL formatting guidelines. Accepted papers will be presented at the workshop and included in the proceedings.

Topics of interest include (but are not limited to):

  • Evaluation of Machine Translation (MT)
  • Evaluation of Natural Language Generation (NLG)
  • Evaluation of Large Language Models (LLMs)
  • Semantic similarity and textual entailment evaluation
  • Evaluation of Question Answering (QA) systems
  • Summarization evaluation (faithfulness, factuality, coherence)
  • Evaluation of dialogue and conversational systems
  • Cross-lingual and code-mixed evaluation
  • Grounded language and multimodal evaluation (speech–text–vision)
  • Hallucination detection and factual consistency
  • Interpretability and explainability in NLP systems
  • Benchmarking and dataset design for evaluation
  • Evaluation of Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)
  • Evaluation of Text-to-Speech (TTS)
  • Speech Translation evaluation
  • Evaluation of Speech-to-Speech systems
  • Speech LLM evaluation
  • Spoken Language Cognition
  • Evaluation metrics beyond WER
  • Human vs automatic evaluation methods
  • Low-resource and multilingual evaluation
  • Evaluation of expressive and prosodic speech
  • Bias and robustness in speech systems

Shared Tasks

EWSLT 2026 introduces benchmark-driven shared tasks to evaluate speech and language technologies in Indian contexts. Each track provides datasets, evaluation protocols, and leaderboards to enable fair and reproducible comparison.

Track 1: Indic Spoken Question Answering (SQA)

Spoken Question Answering (SQA) focuses on answering natural language questions delivered as audio. Participants build systems that take spoken questions as input and generate answers in the same language. This track introduces benchmark datasets for Indic languages (starting with Telugu) and evaluates systems using an LLM-as-Judge framework designed to capture the linguistic richness of Indian languages.

Task Setup
  • Input: Spoken question (audio)
  • Output: Natural language answer
  • Languages: Telugu (others TBA)
Evaluation
  • LLM-as-Judge evaluation framework
  • Factual correctness, fluency, completeness
  • Language appropriateness (code-switch handling)
Participation
  • Build SQA systems (end-to-end or cascaded)
  • Submit predictions on held-out test set
  • Submit system description paper (ACL format)
